The drug that's treating someone you know was probably not made by the company on the label. Somewhere in the supply chain is a lab that ran the synthesis, optimised the process, scaled it up — invisibly, precisely, for decades. Most people have never heard of that lab.

Anthem Biosciences is trying to be that lab, at scale, across every stage of drug development. It IPO'd in July 2025. 67x oversubscribed. Listed at a 27% premium. It has the largest fermentation capacity of any contract manufacturer in India — by a factor of six. And the man who built it bet everything he had on it at 46.

This is me trying to understand whether the premium makes sense. Not investment advice. I'm learning out loud.

## Why I'm Looking at This

India's role in global pharma has been climbing the value chain for years. We started as a generics manufacturer. We built API capacity. The next step is CDMOs — Contract Development and Manufacturing Organisations — where companies don't just manufacture, they help discover and develop drugs too.

Anthem uses the term CRDMO: Contract Research, Development, and Manufacturing Organisation. The added "R" is not marketing. It means they're involved from the moment a pharma company has a promising molecule, not just when someone hands them a recipe to make at scale.

The structural tailwind is real and it's happening now. Global pharma companies are systematically increasing outsourcing. The China+1 strategy is moving manufacturing supply chains out of China. The US BIOSECURE Act, which threatened to restrict American drug companies from working with specific large Chinese CDMOs, accelerated that shift. India — with its cost structure, chemistry talent, and regulatory track record — is the natural destination.

Anthem sits at the premium end of that opportunity.

## What Anthem Biosciences Actually Does

Two businesses under one roof.

**CRDMO services (83% of revenue):** End-to-end drug services — discovery, process development, scale-up, and commercial manufacturing — for global pharma and biotech clients. The client hands over a molecule (or sometimes just a biological target), and Anthem takes it as far down the value chain as the client needs.

Capabilities span: small molecule synthesis, fermentation-based manufacturing, biologics, antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs), RNA interference (RNAi), peptides, flow chemistry, biocatalysis, enzymatic processes, and oligonucleotides. That's a deliberately wide platform covering both established and next-generation therapeutic modalities.

**Speciality Ingredients (17% of revenue):** A portfolio of fermentation and bioprocess-derived products — serratiopeptidase, vitamin B7 (biotin), probiotics, enzymes, nutritional actives, and biosimilars. These are sold directly to pharma and nutraceutical manufacturers rather than on a contract basis. More steady-state revenue, lower growth, slightly different economics.

The client base spans 550+ companies across 50 countries. Anchor clients include AbbVie, Novartis, and Pfizer. Revenue is 85% exports, primarily US and Europe. US marketing runs through DavosPharma, an affiliate that is also a company shareholder — an arrangement worth watching (more on this in the risks section).

All three manufacturing facilities are in Bommasandra and Harohalli, Bengaluru — same geography, which simplifies operations but creates geographic concentration.

## The Story: From Biocon to a ₹42,000 Crore Company

**1980s–2005:** Ajay Bhardwaj studies chemical engineering at IIT Delhi, completes graduate studies at Louisiana State University on fellowship, and spends 20 years at Biocon in Bengaluru. He rises to the senior leadership team, overseeing marketing. When an expected promotion doesn't materialise, he quits.

**2006:** At 46, with two children heading to university, Bhardwaj sells his 1% stake in Biocon, takes a bank loan, and invests roughly $9 million of his own money into Anthem Biosciences.

What he understood: global pharma companies were being pressured by rising R&D costs and declining drug approval rates. Outsourcing the development and manufacturing process — not just the manufacturing — was the answer. India had the chemistry talent. No Indian company was building a comprehensive platform for it.

**2006–2020:** Building from scratch. Fermentation capability — the hardest, most capital-intensive part — was central to the thesis from the beginning. Most labs avoid fermentation because of the complexity. Anthem leaned into it.

**2020–2025:** The growth phase. Revenue compounds at ~32% annually from FY23 to FY25. Commercial molecules increase. The pipeline deepens.

**July 2025:** IPO at ₹570 per share. 67x oversubscribed. Listing price ₹723 — a 27% premium on day one. Ajay Bhardwaj, now 65, appears on Forbes' Billionaires list for the first time with a net worth of $2.4 billion. He is also named EY Entrepreneur of the Year 2025 finalist and is a founder-trustee of Plaksha University.

The $9 million gamble at 46 became $2.4 billion by 65. That's the personal story underneath the company.

## The Fermentation Moat

This is the part most analyses mention but few fully explain.

Fermentation is the use of microorganisms — bacteria, fungi, yeast — to produce a desired compound through a biological process. Many antibiotics were originally made this way. Complex enzymes are made this way. Many next-generation biologics require fermentation expertise. It is the biological equivalent of what traditional chemistry does for small molecules.

**Why fermentation creates a genuine moat:**

Building large-scale fermentation capacity requires significant capital, deep process science, and years of accumulated knowledge about how to run biological processes reliably at commercial scale. You cannot just build the stainless steel tanks. You need scientists who understand the biology, the process controls, the scaling dynamics. That knowledge lives in people and in years of runs, not in blueprints.

Anthem has **142 kL of fermentation capacity** as of March 2025 — the largest among all assessed Indian CRDMOs. With the NeoAnthem Unit III fermentation block coming online, this rises to **182 kL**.

The second-largest Indian CDMO player has approximately 30 kL. Anthem is **six times** the size of the next competitor in this specific capability.

Among global CRDMOs with meaningful fermentation capacity, it is a short list. Among Indian companies, Anthem is in a different category. A competitor can't close that gap in two or three years, or even five.

## The Molecule Flywheel

This is the central compounding mechanism in Anthem's business model, and it's what makes a CRDMO fundamentally different from a contract manufacturer.

Every drug starts as an early-phase molecule. A pharmaceutical company has a promising compound and needs help figuring out how to synthesise it efficiently, develop the manufacturing process, and eventually scale it for commercial production. That work starts small — a few hundred grams, maybe kilograms — and if the drug progresses through clinical trials, the volumes grow dramatically. When the drug reaches commercial launch, the manufacturing contract can run for decades.

Anthem's current pipeline position:

- **130–140 early-phase molecules** across its client base
- **8 molecules in Phase 3 clinical trials** (the last stage before commercial launch)
- **14 commercial molecules** (up from 10 — four were added in FY26)
- Four recently commercialised molecules with a combined **peak market sales estimate of ~$10 billion**
- Six commercial molecules expected to reach a combined **$24 billion in peak sales by CY2029**

That last number is the one that matters for the long-term thesis. Anthem doesn't just grow by winning new projects. Every molecule that progresses through the pipeline increases the volumes Anthem manufactures, and if the drug becomes a blockbuster, that revenue is recurring for decades.

The reverse risk is real too: molecules fail in trials, programmes get terminated, clients switch CDMOs. But with 130+ early-phase molecules and 8 in Phase 3, the funnel is wide. The attrition is priced into the process.

## The Industry

### India CDMO: A Market Set to Double

The India CDMO market is projected at $23.3 billion in 2026, growing to ~$57.9 billion by 2031 — roughly a 14% CAGR. The global CDMO market grows at ~9% CAGR. India is outpacing the world.

**Why India is winning:**

Cost-effective operations (30–40% lower than US or Europe), a large pool of PhD chemists and process engineers, a generics-era manufacturing infrastructure now being upgraded for CDMO work, and government PLI incentives directing capital into pharma manufacturing capacity.

### China+1 and BIOSECURE

This is the macro tailwind specific to the current moment. Global pharma — particularly American pharma — has been reducing dependence on Chinese CDMOs. The US BIOSECURE Act, which targeted large Chinese CRDMOs including Wuxi Apptec and Wuxi Biologics, accelerated this trend. US biotechs began disclosing Chinese CDMO relationships as material risks in filings. Several saw stock declines after announcing new China-based partnerships.

When US pharma needs to diversify away from China, India is the next credible destination at scale. Anthem has existing US client relationships and operates with minimal Chinese raw material dependence. The China+1 shift is structural and will take 5–10 years to fully play out. The revenue impact is just beginning to show.

### The Biologics Shift

APIs and intermediates (small molecules) still account for ~52% of the CDMO market today. But large molecules and biologics are growing at ~15% CAGR — the fastest segment. Fermentation is fundamental to many large molecule manufacturing processes. Anthem's 142 kL fermentation capacity positions it directly in the path of where the industry is heading.

Divi's is India's largest custom synthesis CDMO by revenue. But Divi's has zero capability in fermentation, ADC, RNAi, or peptides. It's a scale giant in traditional small molecule chemistry, not positioned for next-generation therapeutics. Anthem plays there.

Syngene is the most structurally similar listed company — full service, research to manufacturing. But its EBITDA margins (~27%) are 16 percentage points below Anthem's. That gap is real and reflects the mix of higher-margin commercial molecules in Anthem's book.

Only Sai Life Sciences and Anthem have broad enough modality coverage to serve across both established and next-generation drug types. Among the two, Anthem's fermentation depth is the differentiator.

The honest answer: Anthem is structurally unique at its capability breadth-to-size ratio among listed Indian companies. That uniqueness is also what sustains the valuation premium.

## Valuation

This is where it gets uncomfortable.

Anthem trades at approximately **71x trailing PE** on FY26 consolidated PAT of ₹592 crore. Market cap is ~₹42,350 crore at ~₹766 per share (~55.3 crore shares outstanding). On revenue, that's **~20x FY26 revenue** — a technology company multiple applied to a manufacturing business.

For context, Syngene trades at approximately 45–50x trailing PE. Divi's at 55–60x. Anthem commands a premium to both, which requires sustained earnings outperformance to justify.

**Forward picture:**

Management is confident in sustaining the historical 20%+ growth rate but has not issued specific FY27 guidance. At 20% PAT growth, FY27 PAT would be approximately ₹710 crore.

At 60x forward PE — a meaningful compression from current levels — the stock is roughly flat even at 20% earnings growth. Upside requires either earnings growth above 20%, a sustained multiple, or both.

Seven analysts currently rate the stock "Strong Buy" with an average 12-month price target of ₹879 (14.7% upside from ~₹766). Citi initiated with a buy rating and ₹870 target, projecting 19% revenue CAGR through FY27–28.

The bull case for holding the multiple: Anthem's 43.4% EBITDA margins are the highest among listed Indian CRDMOs. As commercial molecules scale and Unit IV comes online, operating leverage means margins can expand further without proportional cost additions. Every new blockbuster molecule is decades of recurring revenue. These aren't one-time wins.

The honest read: this is priced for near-perfection. The valuation already embeds the molecule flywheel, the fermentation moat, the China+1 tailwind, and above-20% growth — simultaneously. A miss on any one of them is painful at 71x.

## The Expansion: NeoAnthem and Unit IV

**NeoAnthem (Unit III):** Largely operational. All blocks except the fermentation unit have been commissioned. The fermentation block was expected by end of FY26/early FY27. Unit III is already generating revenue with a target of ₹100–150 crore by year-end. An additional 76 kL of custom synthesis capacity at Unit II is also being added.

**Unit IV:** The big bet. A 30-acre greenfield facility with a Phase 1 investment of ₹1,200 crore+. Designed to roughly double Anthem's capacities — adding ~400 kL custom synthesis capacity and 100–200 kL additional fermentation. Also includes a dedicated Food and Nutraceutical facility. Phase 1 commissioning is targeted within approximately two years (by ~early FY29). Unit IV will primarily support large-scale commercial manufacturing once the Phase 3 pipeline converts.

The rationale: Anthem's existing capacity can handle the current commercial portfolio. Unit IV is being built for the molecules that are in Phase 3 today and will be commercial by FY28–29. If those molecules convert as expected, Anthem needs the capacity ready. If they're late to build, they lose contracts.

## Bull Case vs Risks

### Why This Could Work

- **Fermentation moat is real and durable.** Six times the next competitor. Two decades of process science. Not something anyone closes in five years.
- **Molecule flywheel is already compounding.** 14 commercial molecules, $24 billion in peak sales from six of them. Every drug that goes commercial is recurring revenue for decades.
- **China+1 tailwind is structural, not cyclical.** The relocation of drug manufacturing away from China will take 5–10 years to fully play out. Anthem is already positioned for US clients through DavosPharma.
- **Q4 FY26 exit rate significantly above full-year average.** Destocking is behind them. The business entered FY27 running at ₹2,444 crore annualised. 20%+ FY27 growth is achievable from here.
- **Margins are expanding, not contracting.** Q4 EBITDA margins at 48.1% vs full-year 43.4%. High-margin commercial molecules are a growing share of revenue.
- **Ajay Bhardwaj's skin in the game is real.** He put his life savings into this business. His net worth is the share price. The Unit IV expansion tells you he is not winding down at 65.
- **95.6% FFS project success rate.** This is not a number that happens by accident. It reflects deep process expertise and a client retention effect.

### What Could Go Wrong

- **Valuation is the primary risk.** At 71x trailing PE, there is limited room for error. The Q3 FY26 destocking quarter showed how quickly sentiment turns at this multiple.
- **DavosPharma concentration.** US marketing is handled by DavosPharma, which is also a company shareholder and was a selling shareholder in the IPO. This is a related-party arrangement that doubles as customer concentration in a critical geography. If the relationship sours, US revenue is disrupted.
- **Unit IV capex execution risk.** A ₹1,200 crore Phase 1 commitment is significant. Delays in commissioning, or demand not filling capacity on schedule, creates an earnings overhang during construction.
- **Molecule attrition.** Not all 8 Phase 3 molecules will reach commercial launch. Not all 130 early-phase molecules will make it through. If the pipeline thins, the flywheel slows.
- **Pricing pressure.** Indian CDMO capacity is expanding rapidly. If the market gets more competitive — from Sai Life, Neuland, or new entrants — pricing can compress.
- **US pharma tariff risk.** With 85% revenue from exports and the bulk from the US, any import tariff on pharmaceutical products is a direct earnings headwind. This risk is discussed but not resolved.
- **Speciality Ingredients is a drag.** At 8% YoY growth in Q4 FY26, this segment is growing well below the CRDMO business. If the higher-growth CRDMO segment also slows, the blended growth number deteriorates.

## My Take

The core question with Anthem is not whether the business is good. It clearly is: 43% EBITDA margins that are still expanding, India's largest fermentation capacity by a factor of six, a pipeline with genuine blockbuster exposure across 130+ molecules, and a founder who built this from scratch with his own money and his own conviction.

What gives me pause is the valuation. At 71x trailing PE, the stock is priced assuming a lot goes right: the Phase 3 molecules convert, Unit IV commissions on time, BIOSECURE/China+1 flows to Anthem's US clients, and margins hold above 40%. These are plausible outcomes. They are not guaranteed outcomes.

The way I think about the molecule flywheel: it creates a kind of earnings visibility that isn't captured in a trailing PE number. When a drug reaches commercial scale, Anthem's revenue from that molecule doesn't stop — it compounds as the drug sells. That's different from winning one-time manufacturing tenders. It's closer to a royalty stream embedded in a manufacturing contract. That visibility deserves some premium.

If the Phase 3 pipeline converts and Unit IV comes online without significant delay, this could look like a substantially different business in three years — one where the Q4 48.1% EBITDA margin is the floor rather than a high watermark.

I've been watching since the IPO. The destocking quarter scared some investors away. Q4 recovered sharply. The real test is FY27 growth and whether Unit IV stays on schedule. Those are the two variables I'm tracking.

I don't hold this yet. The valuation gives me pause. But I'm not ruling it out.

None of this is investment advice. I am learning out loud.
